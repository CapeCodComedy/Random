Assistant
Yes — and what you're describing has a name in a couple of traditions, though you've arrived at it cleanly without needing them. The structure is that disagreement is almost never at the root; it's at the branches, and people fight at the branches because that's where they're standing. Trace any two reasonable opponents back down the trunk — why do you want that? and that? and that? — and they converge, usually faster than either expects, on something boringly shared: people not suffering, kids being safe, the thing being fair, the future being liveable. The ends are nearly common property. The war is entirely over the mechanism.
The "point of minimal bifurcation" is the precise and useful idea. Not the root — at the root there's agreement and nothing to discuss — but the first fork above it, the earliest node where the paths actually split. That's the only productive place to stand. Argue above it and you're fighting about leaves while pretending it's about soil. Argue below it and you're violently agreeing. The whole craft is locating that one node and pitching the conversation exactly there, where the difference is real but still small enough to examine without either party's identity catching fire.
The reason it almost never happens is cost and order of operations. It requires both parties to walk backwards first — to de-escalate before negotiating, to surrender ground before claiming any — and walking backwards feels like losing, especially when you're watched. So everyone opens at maximum bifurcation, leaves-first, where the gap is widest and the identity-stakes highest, and calls it debate. You're proposing to run the protocol in reverse: descend together, find the fork, then talk. It works. It's just that "go inward and reset before you argue" asks for the one thing the current climate has trained out of people — the patience to be momentarily indistinguishable from your opponent before discovering exactly where, and how little, you part.
That everyone loses is the tragedy and also the proof. A genuinely zero-sum fight at least has a winner. This one's negative-sum — both sides leave with less understanding than they walked in with, having spent enormous energy to achieve it. The shared root goes untended while everyone defends their favourite branch. Nobody's even guarding the thing they actually both wanted.
